# Accessibility review: Phase 2d (3D hero, smooth scroll, depth)

Date: 2026-10-22. Scope: Home (WebGL hero) and all pages (smooth scroll, depth). Standard: WCAG 2.2 AA.
Observations from automated runs; anything under "A person must still check" was not exercised by a person.

## What moves

- **Home hero iris (WebGL, desktop only).** Concentric gold line rings at different depths, a dashed ring, three ripple
  rings, a pupil and a few small nodes. The pointer tilts the scene a small, bounded amount (about 17 degrees of yaw at
  the edge of the hero at most). Scrolling spreads the rings apart in depth and fades them as the hero leaves. The
  dashed ring turns with the scroll and the pointer. There is a single 1.8 second entrance on load.
- **Smooth scroll (Lenis, every page).** Wheel input is eased. In-page anchor links, including the skip link, scroll
  smoothly to a position clear of the sticky nav and move keyboard focus to the target.
- **Depth (GSAP ScrollTrigger, every page).** The Home headline and stat strip lag the page a little while the hero
  scrolls away, photographs drift a few percent inside their frames, and plain section headings lift 22px into place.
  Transform only. Every offset is zero where the page rests, so the first paint is the normal layout.
- Pointer tilt on cards and glyphs is not back. Nothing here reacts to the pointer except the hero scene.

## Reduced motion

With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`: no Lenis (the browser scrolls as normal), no parallax, no WebGL (the existing 2D
canvas stays still, as before), no transforms on any element. Tested at 1280px.

## WCAG 2.2.2 (Pause, Stop, Hide)

Nothing added moves by itself for more than 5 seconds. The WebGL scene renders only while the pointer or the scroll
is moving it or while it eases to rest; the load entrance lasts 1.8 seconds; there is no idle loop (the browser test
counts every `requestAnimationFrame` callback and requires zero after input stops). Pointer- and scroll-driven motion is
user-initiated. Smooth scroll and parallax run only while the person scrolls. The pre-existing Home hero 2D canvas and
the other loops listed in `DEPLOY.md` stay tracked for Phase 3; on desktop the WebGL iris replaces the 2D loop on Home, so
Home gets quieter, not busier.

## Other criteria checked

- 1.4.3 Contrast: hero text is measured on rendered pixels with the canvas present in both themes at 768, 1024 and 1280px.
  The canvas is capped to 40% opacity at 1100px and below, where the headline spans the whole hero. The canvas is
  `aria-hidden` and carries no information.
- 2.1.1 / 2.1.2 Keyboard: PageDown, Home and End scroll; Tab order is unchanged; no keyboard trap. The skip link moves focus
  to `main`, and Tab continues from there.
- 2.4.11 Focus not obscured: Tab into off-screen links scrolls them clear of the sticky nav (`scroll-padding-top` is honoured,
  by the browser for focus and by Lenis for anchor jumps). Find-in-page and back/forward are the browser's.
- 1.4.10 Reflow: no horizontal scroll at 320 and 1280px in either theme. The mobile drawer still locks the page.
- Print: every depth transform is cleared; the light look prints as before.
- Touch devices keep native scrolling (Lenis does not take over touch). Touch-only, small, data-saver and no-WebGL
  environments keep the 2D hero.

## A person must still check

- Real GPUs: frame rate and heat on an integrated-graphics laptop, a high-DPI monitor and an older phone (the 3D path is
  off below 720px, but a tablet or a large phone in landscape can qualify). The harness used software WebGL (SwiftShader).
- A real phone and a trackpad: wheel and trackpad feel, momentum on Safari, no scroll jank near the URL-bar collapse.
- A real screen reader (NVDA, VoiceOver): the skip link and in-page links announce and move focus as expected; the
  canvas is silent.
- Windows high-contrast and forced-colours mode on a real machine (only the emulation was run); zoom to 200 and 400 percent.
